> **AI should coach the user—not do the thinking for them.**

**Core loop:**

> **Think → Speak → Analyze → Correct → Retry → Progress**

---

# 1. The Product Thesis

## 1.1 The problem

Modern communication has a strange asymmetry.

People have unprecedented access to information, writing assistance and AI-generated answers, yet many struggle to:

* formulate thoughts quickly
* retrieve vocabulary while speaking
* communicate ideas coherently
* explain complex subjects simply
* speak spontaneously
* organize arguments
* avoid filler words
* maintain a natural pace
* communicate under pressure
* sound confident despite knowing the material

Traditional solutions aren't particularly convenient.

### Option A — Practice alone

You don't know what you're doing wrong.

### Option B — Watch YouTube

You consume information rather than practice.

### Option C — Toastmasters

Excellent human feedback and structured practice, but requires scheduled participation and isn't infinitely available.

Toastmasters remains an enormous validation of the category: it currently has more than 14,000 clubs worldwide, and its core model is structured speaking, impromptu responses and peer evaluation. ([Toastmasters International][2])

### Option D — AI speech coach

Convenient, but generally focused on analyzing **how you spoke**.

Orai, for example, already provides real-time feedback around fillers, pace, clarity and energy, while Speeko analyzes areas such as pace, tone, intonation, sentiment, talk time and word choice. ([Orai][1])

### Option E — Ask ChatGPT

You can get an excellent answer.

But then **the AI did the thinking.**

---

# 2. Oratry's fundamental insight

The problem isn't simply:

> **"People don't know how to speak."**

It's:

> **People don't practice the entire cognitive process that happens immediately before and during speaking.**

The pipeline is:

```text
INFORMATION
     ↓
UNDERSTANDING
     ↓
THINKING
     ↓
FORMULATING
     ↓
STRUCTURING
     ↓
WORD RETRIEVAL
     ↓
SPEAKING
     ↓
DELIVERY
```

Existing speech coaches concentrate heavily on the bottom:

```text
                    Oratry
                       ↓
Information → Thinking → Structure → Language → Delivery
                                      ↑
                         Existing speech coaching
```

**That is our opportunity.**

---

# 3. The Oratry promise

I would make the promise incredibly simple:

> ### **Think better. Speak better.**

And the product explanation:

> **Oratry gives you short, progressively difficult challenges that force you to research, think, formulate and communicate ideas under pressure. AI analyzes your performance, identifies what is holding you back, and builds your next challenge around it.**

The critical phrase is:

# **"builds your next challenge around it."**

That's what turns a collection of AI features into a training system.

At a high level:

```text
                  ┌───────────────┐
                  │   WEB CLIENT  │
                  │ React / Next  │
                  └───────┬───────┘
                          │
                          ▼
                  ┌───────────────┐
                  │    BACKEND    │
                  │ API + Auth    │
                  └───────┬───────┘
                          │
             ┌────────────┼────────────┐
             ▼            ▼            ▼
         Challenge      Speech       User
         Engine         Engine       Profile
             │            │            │
             ▼            ▼            ▼
         Topic/LLM       STT +       Skill Graph
                         Analysis
             │            │            │
             └────────────┼────────────┘
                          ▼
                  ┌───────────────┐
                  │ COACHING      │
                  │ ENGINE        │
                  └───────┬───────┘
                          │
                          ▼
                  Next Exercise
```

The important architectural decision:

# **Don't make the LLM the entire application.**

Use deterministic systems where deterministic systems work.

For example:

### WPM

Calculate it.

Don't ask an LLM.

### Filler count

Detect it programmatically.

### Target-word usage

Programmatic + semantic validation.

### Speech structure

LLM/NLP.

### Coaching

LLM.

### Curriculum selection

Rules initially → eventually ML/recommendation model.

That makes the system much more reliable.

# 23. Scoring Architecture

This is one area I want us to be intellectually rigorous about.

Don't pretend:

> "AI knows you were 72% eloquent."

That's bullshit.

Instead classify feedback into:

### Objective

> You spoke 147 words/minute.

> You used "um" 11 times.

> Your longest pause was 3.1 seconds.

These are measurable.

---

### Model-derived

> Your response appears to contain three major arguments.

> Your conclusion doesn't clearly resolve your opening claim.

These are estimates.

---

### Coaching judgment

> Your second argument would be stronger if you introduced evidence before moving to the counterargument.

This is where the LLM is useful.

The UI should distinguish these conceptually even if not explicitly labeling every item.

# 7. STT output

Don't store only a plain transcript.

We want **word-level timestamps**.

Conceptually:

```json

  "start": 12.32,
  "end": 12.71,
  "confidence": 0.97
}
```

This allows us to calculate:

* speaking rate
* pauses
* fillers
* word retrieval latency
* timing
* interruptions
* target-word usage

The word timestamps are far more valuable than simply:

> "Here's your transcript."

# 8. Deterministic speech metrics

These should NOT be delegated to an LLM.

Calculate:

### WPM

```text
spoken words / speaking minutes
```

### Filler rate

```text
filler words / total words
```

### Pause frequency

Count pauses over a threshold.

### Long pauses

For example:

> > 1.5 seconds

### Repetition

Detect repeated words/phrases.

### Self-correction

Patterns such as:

> "The government—actually, I mean..."

### Speech duration

Exact.

### Target word usage

Exact detection.

# 10. Scoring Methodology

This deserves serious thought because **bad scoring can destroy user trust.**

I would have five V1 dimensions.

---

## 1. Structure — 25%

Did the speech have a logical progression?

Evaluate:

* clear opening
* central idea
* sequencing
* transitions
* conclusion
* logical consistency

---

## 2. Clarity — 20%

Can the listener easily understand the ideas?

Evaluate:

* sentence complexity
* ambiguity
* unnecessary repetition
* unclear references
* explanation quality

---

## 3. Fluency — 20%

Objective-ish.

Measure:

* fillers
* hesitation
* pauses
* repetitions
* self-correction

---

## 4. Language — 20%

Measure:

* vocabulary diversity
* precision
* target words
* appropriate usage
* unnecessary verbal clutter

---

## 5. Delivery — 15%

Measure:

* pace
* pacing consistency
* vocal variation
* pauses
* emphasis

---

# Overall score

```text
Overall =
Structure × .25
+ Clarity × .20
+ Fluency × .20
+ Language × .20
+ Delivery × .15
```

But I'd actually **avoid presenting this formula to users.**

Internally it provides consistency.

---

# 11. Don't score everything against an imaginary "perfect speaker"

Instead, establish:

### Baseline

User's initial performance.

Then:

### Personal improvement

Current performance vs baseline.

This allows:

> **74**

and:

> **+11 since you started**

Both matter.

# 12. Confidence intervals / score reliability

This is something I'd build into the backend even if users never see it.

> Structure = model judgment.

```json
  "score": 72,
  "confidence": 0.84
}
```

This allows us later to avoid overreacting to noisy evaluations.

```text
IF filler_rate > target
THEN fluency_training = HIGH
```

Then:

```text
IF vocabulary_retrieval < 60
THEN vocabulary_training = HIGH
```

Then:

```text
IF same skill trained 3 times
AND score improves
THEN increase difficulty
```

Simple.

Explainable.

Debuggable.

# 22. Challenge recommendation algorithm

Conceptually:

```text
candidate challenges
        ↓
remove recently completed
        ↓
remove too-easy
        ↓
remove too-hard
        ↓
prioritize weak skills
        ↓
consider user's goals
        ↓
consider variety
        ↓
score candidates
        ↓
select best challenge
```

Candidate score:

```text
Recommendation Score =

Skill Need
+ Goal Relevance
+ Appropriate Difficulty
+ Variety
+ Vocabulary Relevance
+ Recency
```

Eventually ML can replace this.

Not now.

# 46. One architectural decision I would strongly recommend

There should be **two separate feedback layers**.

### Layer 1 — Measurement

> "You spoke 154 WPM."

> "You used 12 fillers."

> "You paused >2 seconds 4 times."

Objective.

---

### Layer 2 — Interpretation

> "Your pace increased when you discussed your second argument, which made that section harder to follow."

AI-derived.

This separation gives us:

**trust + intelligence.**

If the LLM hallucinates something, we can still trust the underlying measurements.

---

# 47. One more important design principle: feedback should be evidence-based

Never allow the AI to say:

> "You sounded nervous."

Instead:

> "Your speaking rate increased from 139 WPM in the first minute to 172 WPM in the final minute."

Then potentially:

> "This may indicate that you were rushing toward the end."

The first is a diagnosis.

The second is an observation.

That's a much more defensible product.
